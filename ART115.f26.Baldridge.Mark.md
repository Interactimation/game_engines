## Introduction

**From the catalog:**

> A time-based medium, animation adopts ideas and approaches from graphic design and performance to create the illusion of motion, define characters and tell stories. Tools and techniques of 2D animation (which may include approaches to 3D) will be explored by students in this course.

We'll take a key word from the description above and define animation as methods for creating an:  
_Illusion of Life_

Within this context we'll explore:  

* **Rhythm**  

Visual or sonic, in space or time, rhythm provides structure —it's one of the most dynamic principles of design    
The exploration of rhythm pays dividends in many fields of creative activity

* **Character**  

From ["flour sacks"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMr5p3iWW4Q) and other abstract shapes, up to photorealistic human beings, character comes down to personality, a sense of autonomy, goal-seeking behavior 

* **Meaning**   

Feeling is first. This is what we talk of when referring to the "expressiveness" of a medium  
_Intention, messaging, advertising?_ Feeling is the carrier wave on which they ride

## Goals and Objectives

### Instructional Goals: 

* Build a foundation in the fundamentals of animation
* Explore the relation between character and narrative 
* Learn to tell stories with animation

### Learning Objectives

* **Technical Proficiency** Move shapes and colors around in space, demonstrating spacial and temporal rhythm
* **Conceptual** Create and animate characters with personality, discover how character _generates_ narrative
* **Developmental** Learn to control emotional effects to create satisfying experiences

### Projects

In this course we will produce three Projects

* **Abstractions** gives us a chance to play with rhythm in space and time, to experiment with textures and sync sound 

* In **Characters tell Stories** we'll learn that contrasting characters in carefully chosen situations generate stories on their own

* **Advance!** Gives us a chance to try out some new techniques, more fully develop our ideas and projects or introduce new technologies or softwares (This may include interactivity, depending on student interest)

## Evaluation

All course work will be evaluated on the basis of a 100-point semester

* **Tutorial Assignments** 
    * Each of these is calculated to lead to a particular Project
    * The number of these may vary, but for each of the three sections, assignments will always total 10 points (if there are five Tutorial Assignments leading up to _Abstractions_, each will be worth 2 points, etc)   

* **Three Projects** 
    * As described and named above
    * Each worth a maximum of 20 points

* **The remaining 10 points are divided into:** 

    * _Creativity Points_ (3) 
        * Show design-centric work which engages viewers on an aesthetic level 
    * _Innovation Points_ (3)
        * Demonstrate techniques or approaches not covered in class, seek to educate yourself beyond the coursework and demonstrate that knowledge 
    * _Engagement Points_ (4)
        * Show up to and engage in classroom conversations, ask and answer questions, _help your peers_ (this last is always the most impressive to your instructor)

### Examples:

Let's take our Project One: _Abstractions_ as an example of how all three projects may be developed and evaluated:

> The project is worth a maximum of 20 points

As a means of exploration and skill building, and in preparation for the Project, a set of Tutorial Assignments in animated GIFs, sync sound or etc will be set

> Tutorial assignments for this section, whatever their number, will be worth 10 points, total

**Tutorial Assignments** are evaluated by _completion_: to perform them _as described_ earns full points

**Projects** must be completed to a higher standard and demand a learner's best efforts in design thinking, execution and expressiveness

A close reading of the above reveals that three Projects and all supporting tutorial assignments are worth only a total of 90 points —a further 10 points are available through three channels:
The remaining 10 points are divided into:

**• 3 Creativity points** 
**• 3 Innovation points** 
**• 4 Engagement points** 

Which accrue throughout the semester

> If, early on, you have only one Creativity point, for example, be aware that this grade will not be complete until the semester ends!
